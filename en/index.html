Reviewing your
contribution rate

Occupational contingencies. Which rate genuinely applies to each person, and how it is corrected going forward and backward.

An unreviewed classification is not just a cost. It is also an exposure.

The premium schedule has two tables, one giving a rate by economic activity and another giving it by occupation. When a person's occupation appears in the second, it overrides the activity of the whole company.

Workforces change, roles move, and the classification stays as it was on day one. When a company under-declares, the exposure stays open for four years, and when it over-declares, it contributes every month above what it owes.

At Ayming we believe no company should be left exposed, or pay more than it owes, because of a classification nobody has looked at since.

Why now

Deadline running
Before 1 February 2027

Every Spanish company reports to the Tesorería the occupation code of every person already registered, within a six month window opened on 1 August 2026. From that date the authorities hold, for the entire workforce, a declaration of what each employee does.

Sole additional provision of Real Decreto 643/2026 of 29 July
Already in force
Since 1 January 2026

A company that did not report its CNAE-2025 code receives the highest rate among the compatible codes, and a late report only takes effect from the following settlement period, never retroactively.

Forty fifth transitional provision of the LGSS. No court has ruled on the transition yet, so this is a check to run, not a finding.

Said precisely, because it can be checked in the BOE, that occupation code is not the clave of Cuadro II and does not by itself change the contribution rate. What it does is put the question on the table, for the whole workforce, with a date attached.

How this gets handled today

Not looking

Not looking is not neutral. The direct settlement system proposes each month and the company confirms, with nobody reviewing the occupation claves entered on the day of registration. Under-declaring leaves the risk open for four years, over-declaring loses the saving payment by payment.

Asking the payroll adviser

Outside their remit. The adviser applies the claves the company gives them, rigorously, and their brief is payroll. Cross-checking actual duties, the real place of work and Tribunal Supremo case law is a different specialism, and nobody has asked them for it.

The law firm

Defends a position somebody sets beforehand. It argues well and carries the proceedings when they are needed, but cross-checking payroll, the real place of work and actual duties comes before the legal argument.

Questions we get asked

If we start looking at this, are we not inviting an inspection?

The exposure already exists and runs whether it is looked at or not, and the authorities have their own four year window to assess. Quantifying it privately, in both directions, is what makes it manageable, and the decision to file always rests with the client.

And if it does not hold?

Part of the case law has gone against companies, and we say so before anyone else does. That is why the work starts with the evidence file, not with the size of the amount.

How much are we going to recover?

We do not know before seeing the data, and any figure given beforehand would be an estimate dressed up as a result. What we do know is the mechanics, the difference in points multiplied by the real bases, by the people who meet the criterion and by the years still open.

How much work is this going to cost us?

We request the contribution files and work them ourselves, with the corresponding authorisation. From the company we need the material describing the real duties and the place of work, the one thing no Seguridad Social file records.
